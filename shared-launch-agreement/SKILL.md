# Shared Launch Agreement

**What it does:** It gives Product, Sales, and Marketing one process, one agreed story, and one list of claims for every launch, so the website, sales deck, demo, and help docs all say the same thing, and launches stop stretching to 12–16 weeks.

**When to use it:**
- **Full version:** Tier 1 launches, positioning or messaging refreshes, pricing or packaging changes, and anything that changes how Sales describes the product.
- **Light version:** Tier 2 launches. Fill in the kickoff agreement, add new claims to the claim source list, and run the day-30 consistency check on the two assets that matter most.
- **Skip it:** Tier 3 updates and bug fixes. Add any changed claim to the claim source list and move on.

**Run the launch process map once,** then reuse it for every launch. Revisit it after the timeline review, or when the team structure changes.

This skill works inside the meetings and tools you already have. It doesn't add a new meeting where an existing one can carry the work: the kickoff agreement goes in the product kickoff, the sign-off goes in an existing leadership slot, and the claim list lives wherever your team already keeps shared docs.

If your team has its own rules for sources or claims, use them alongside this skill.

## The process

| Step | What happens | File |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Map the process (once) | Write down one launch process all three teams share: who decides what, when each team hands off, and what "ready" means for each | [launch-process-map.md](launch-process-map.md) |
| 1. Agree at kickoff | At the meeting where Product hands off what's being built, all three teams fill in one page: what it does, who it's for, what it doesn't do yet, the main claim, and who decides each open question | [kickoff-agreement.md](kickoff-agreement.md) |
| 2. Test the story together | Product, Sales, and Marketing leaders test the positioning against real deals and the product's real limits, then sign off | [positioning-sign-off.md](positioning-sign-off.md) |
| 3. Build from one source | Every claim goes into one list with its proof and the product owner who confirmed it; every asset pulls from it | [claim-source-list.md](claim-source-list.md) |
| 4. Check and shorten | At day 30, compare the website, deck, demo, and docs side by side and fix gaps at the source; after each launch, cut the step that stalled | [consistency-check.md](consistency-check.md), [launch-timeline-review.md](launch-timeline-review.md) |

## Who owns what

| Team | Owns |
|---|---|
| Product | What the product does and doesn't do, the ship date, and confirming every product claim is true today |
| Sales | Whether the story works in a real deal, and whether reps use it |
| Marketing (PMM) | Running the process, writing the story, the claim source list, and the consistency check |
| All three | The kickoff agreement and the positioning sign-off |

PMM runs the process; it doesn't decide alone. If PMM is the only team that has signed something, it isn't agreed yet.

---

## Self-serve and smaller teams

- **Self-serve or product-led:** Swap "Sales" for whoever owns conversion, usually Growth or the in-product team. Test the positioning against signup flows, onboarding emails, and in-app messages instead of deals, and add the pricing page and onboarding emails to the consistency check.
- **CS-led launches:** Swap "Sales" for the CS leader. Test the story against renewal and onboarding calls.
- **Small teams (one PM, one seller, one marketer):** Run the kickoff agreement and the sign-off as one 45-minute session. Keep the decision record inside the kickoff agreement instead of a separate log.

---

## Ground rules across every file

- **Agree before anything is built,** while changes are still cheap.
- **One story, one claim list.** Every asset pulls from it; nobody writes claims from memory.
- **Every product claim has a named product owner** who confirmed it's true today.
- **If Sales wouldn't say it on a call, it isn't done.**
- **Decisions get written down once,** with who made them. A decision only reopens when the facts change.
- **Every open question has an owner and a date.**
- **Fix gaps at the source,** not one asset at a time.
- **Use the meetings you already have** before adding new ones.

---

## Common mistakes this skill prevents

- Three teams running three launch plans that only meet on launch day.
- Marketing writing the positioning alone, then "presenting" it to Sales and Product.
- Getting what the product does from Product two weeks before launch, after the assets are drafted.
- Approving positioning on slides, without testing it against a real deal.
- Claims nobody can trace to proof, or that were true last quarter.
- The website, deck, demo, and help docs each describing the product, the price, or the audience differently.
- Fixing one asset at a time instead of the claim that caused the gap.
- Reopening the product name, price, or audience in week ten because nobody wrote down who decided.
- Disagreements sitting in a thread for weeks with no one to settle them.
- Adding review rounds to every launch instead of cutting the step that keeps stalling.
