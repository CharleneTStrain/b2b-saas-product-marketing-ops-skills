# Claim Source List

Part of the [shared launch agreement](SKILL.md). Step 3: keep one list of every claim the company makes, with the proof behind it and the product owner who confirmed it's true today.

Websites, decks, and demos contradict each other because each one was written from someone's memory of the story. When every asset pulls its claims from one list, they stop drifting apart, and when a claim changes, you know every place it lives.

---

## What counts as a claim

Anything a buyer could hold you to:
- What the product does ("builds a report in five minutes")
- Numbers ("cuts reporting time by 80%")
- Comparisons ("faster than Competitor X")
- Who it's for ("built for mid-market ops teams")
- Integrations, security, and compliance ("connects to Salesforce")
- Pricing and what's included ("included in Pro and above")

Taglines and brand lines aren't claims unless they promise something specific.

---

## Steps

### 1. Start with the launch, not the whole company

Add the claims from the [kickoff agreement](kickoff-agreement.md) and the [positioning sign-off](positioning-sign-off.md) first. Add existing company-wide claims as you run into them in the consistency check, rather than auditing everything up front.

### 2. Fill in one row per claim

Write the exact approved wording. If there's a short version and a long version, list both.

### 3. Get each product claim confirmed

The product owner confirms it's true today, by name and date. Comparisons and numbers also need a source someone outside the company could check.

### 4. Point every asset at the list

Anyone writing the website, deck, demo script, email, or help docs copies claims from the list instead of writing their own. If they need a claim that isn't on the list, they request it; they don't make it up.

### 5. Update when things change

When a release, price change, or competitor move changes a claim, update the row and list every asset that uses it, so each owner can fix theirs. Flag the change to the [freshness cycle](../rep-ready-content-loop/freshness-cycle.md) for sales cards.

## Template: claim source list

| Claim (approved wording) | Type | Proof | Source | Confirmed by | Date confirmed | Limits | Used in | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build a shareable report in about five minutes | Capability | Beta: median 6 min across 12 accounts | Usage data, [link] | [PM name] | May 22 | Accounts under 50 users | Website, deck, demo, launch kit | Confirmed |
| Included in Pro and above | Pricing | Pricing page update | [pricing owner] | [name] | May 20 | — | Website, deck | Confirmed |
| Faster reporting than Competitor X | Comparison | — | — | — | — | — | — | Needs proof |

**Statuses:**
- **Confirmed:** true today, with proof and a named owner. Safe to use.
- **To confirm (by: ___):** waiting on the product owner or a source.
- **Needs proof:** we believe it, but can't back it up yet. Don't use it.
- **Changed (on: ___):** the wording or facts changed; update every asset in "Used in."
- **Retired:** no longer true or no longer used. Keep the row so nobody adds it back.

---

## Rules

- **Only Confirmed claims go in external assets.**
- **Every product claim has a named product owner** who confirmed it's true today.
- **Every number and comparison has a source** someone outside the company could check.
- **Write limits next to the claim,** so nobody uses it for an audience it isn't true for.
- **One list.** Not one per team, not one per launch. Launches add rows to the same list.
- **Recheck product claims** within five business days of any release that changes them.
- **"Used in" is required.** A claim you can't trace to its assets can't be updated.
