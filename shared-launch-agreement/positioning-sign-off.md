# Positioning Sign-Off

Part of the [shared launch agreement](SKILL.md). Step 2: Product, Sales, and Marketing leaders test the positioning against real deals and the product's real limits, then sign off.

Positioning that only marketing believes usually skipped this step. It was approved on slides, by people who never tested whether a rep could say it on a call or whether the product backs it up. This session tests it before any asset is built.

---

## Who's in the room

- **Product:** the PM for the launch, who knows what it does and doesn't do.
- **Sales:** a sales leader, plus one top rep who sells to the audience.
- **Marketing:** PMM, who runs the session and writes the positioning.
- **Optional:** a CS lead, if existing customers are part of the audience.

Put it on an existing leadership or launch meeting's agenda if you can. It takes 45 to 60 minutes.

---

## Before the session

1. **Draft the positioning** from the kickoff agreement: who it's for, the problem, the main claim, why us over the alternative, and the proof.
2. **Pick three or four real deals** with the audience, from the [win/loss revenue tagger](../pmm-impact-tracker/win-loss-revenue-tagger.md) or the CRM. Include at least one loss and one deal where the buyer chose a competitor or stayed with what they had.
3. **Send both a day ahead,** so people come with reactions instead of reading in the room.

---

## The session

### Test 1: Real deals (20 minutes)

For each deal, ask:
1. "Would this positioning have changed what happened in this deal?"
2. "Where in the deal would a rep say the main claim?"
3. "What would the buyer have said back?"

If the answer to the first question is "no" for every deal, the positioning is aimed at the wrong problem or the wrong audience.

### Test 2: The rep test (10 minutes)

Ask the top rep to say the main claim out loud, in their own words, as if to a buyer. Then ask: "Would you actually say that on a call?"

If they wouldn't, rewrite it together until they would. Keep their wording; it'll be closer to how buyers talk than the draft.

### Test 3: The product test (10 minutes)

Ask the PM, claim by claim: "Is this true today, for this audience, without workarounds?"

| Claim | True today? | For this audience? | Without workarounds? | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reporting in five minutes | Yes | Yes, for accounts under 50 users | Yes | Beta usage data |
| Matches what leadership sees | Partly | Yes | Needs the admin to connect the data source | To confirm |

Anything that isn't a clear yes on all three gets reworded, narrowed, or cut.

### Test 4: Sign off (5 minutes)

Each leader signs off by name, or says exactly what has to change first. Write down the changes, who makes them, and by when.

---

## Async version (when leaders won't meet)

Use this when you can't get all three leaders in a room within a week. It takes each leader about 15 minutes.

1. **Run the tests at the working level first.** Do the real-deal and rep tests with a sales manager and one rep, and the product test with the PM, in two short calls.
2. **Send one page to the three leaders:** the final positioning, the deals tested, what changed from the draft and why, and any claims cut in the product test.
3. **Ask for a reply by a set date,** usually three business days: "Yes," "Yes, if ___," or "No, because ___."
4. **No reply by the date counts as yes.** Say that in the message, so silence doesn't stall the launch.
5. **A "No" goes to the [tie-break rule](tie-break-rule.md).**

## Rules

- **Test against real deals, not hypothetical buyers.**
- **If Sales wouldn't say it on a call, it isn't done.**
- **Every product claim passes the product test,** or it doesn't ship.
- **"Yes, if" counts as agreement** only once the "if" is done. Track it as an open item.
- **No sign-off within a week** goes to the [tie-break rule](tie-break-rule.md).
- **After sign-off,** move the final claims into the [claim source list](claim-source-list.md) as Confirmed, and log the sign-off in the [decision record](decision-record.md).
