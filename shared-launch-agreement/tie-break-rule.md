# Tie-Break Rule

Part of the [shared launch agreement](SKILL.md). Runs alongside the process: agree up front who settles a disagreement between Product, Sales, and Marketing, and how fast.

Disagreements aren't the problem. Disagreements with no way to end them are. Without a rule, they sit in a thread for weeks, the launch date slips, and the loudest person wins by default.

---

## Steps

### 1. Agree the rule once, before any launch

Get the Product, Sales, and Marketing leaders to agree on this when you set up the [launch process map](launch-process-map.md). Agreeing in advance, when nobody is arguing yet, is what makes it work later.

### 2. Try to settle it at the working level first

The people closest to the work (the PM, a sales manager, and PMM) get two business days to agree. They write down both positions in the template below, so the leaders can decide quickly if it goes up.

### 3. Escalate on a set clock

If it isn't settled in two business days, it goes to the tie-breaker. The tie-breaker decides within two more business days, using the written positions. No new meeting is needed unless the tie-breaker asks for one.

### 4. Log the outcome

Add the decision to the [decision record](decision-record.md) with the tie-breaker as decider. Everyone commits to it, including the side that didn't win.

## Who breaks the tie

| Type of disagreement | Tie-breaker | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What the product does or when it ships | Head of Product | Sales wants to say it works for enterprise; Product says it doesn't yet |
| Positioning, audience, or main claim (Tier 1) | Head of Marketing, or whoever PMM reports to | Product wants to lead with a feature; Sales wants to lead with a problem |
| Positioning, wording, or naming (Tier 2 and 3) | PMM decides, after hearing both sides | Two names for a Tier 2 feature |
| Price and packaging | [your pricing owner] | Sales wants it free in every plan; Product wants it in Pro only |
| Whether Sales is ready | Head of Sales | PMM wants to launch Monday; Sales wants another week of training |
| The launch date versus what's in the launch, on a Tier 1 launch | CEO, or the exec all three report to | Ship on the date without a must-have feature, or move the date |

Change the names to fit your company. Each type of disagreement gets one person.

**What doesn't escalate:**
- **Tier 2 and 3 disagreements** stop at PMM or the PM, depending on the type. They don't go to a department head.
- **Wording and naming** stop at PMM unless the name is a Tier 1 product or plan name.
- **The CEO only breaks date-versus-scope ties on Tier 1 launches.** Anything else that reaches the CEO goes back to the right tie-breaker in this table. If it can't be placed in the table, fix the table.

## Template: disagreement summary

```
LAUNCH: [name] · Raised: [date] · Decide by: [date + 2 business days]
TYPE: [product / positioning / price / sales readiness / cross-team]
TIE-BREAKER: [name]

THE QUESTION: ___

POSITION A ([team, name]): ___
  Why: ___
  What it costs if we're wrong: ___

POSITION B ([team, name]): ___
  Why: ___
  What it costs if we're wrong: ___

WHAT WE AGREE ON: ___

DECISION: ___ · Decided by: ___ · Date: ___
```

## Rules

- **Agree the rule before the first disagreement,** not during it.
- **Only Tier 1 disagreements reach a department head,** and only date-versus-scope reaches the CEO.
- **Two business days at the working level, two at the tie-breaker.** Four days, then it's done.
- **Write both positions down,** in the other side's best terms. A tie-breaker can't decide well from one side's summary.
- **"What it costs if we're wrong"** is the most useful line. It usually makes the decision obvious.
- **Disagree and commit.** Once it's decided, nobody relitigates it in the assets or in deals.
- **If the same kind of disagreement keeps coming up,** fix the [launch process map](launch-process-map.md); a decision owner is probably missing.
