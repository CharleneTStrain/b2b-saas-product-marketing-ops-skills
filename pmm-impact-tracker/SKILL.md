Use whenever a PMM needs to answer "how do you know it's working?" for a launch, messaging refresh, pricing change, battlecard set, research project, or enablement program.

# PMM Impact Tracker

**What it does:** It gives every PMM project a target before work starts, a running record of evidence while it's live, a monthly rollup for leadership, and a short readout at the end, so "how do you know it's working?" always has an answer.

**When to use it:**
- **Full version:** Tier 1 launches, messaging refreshes, pricing or packaging changes, and research or enablement programs that should change what Sales says, what buyers do, or what users do in the product.
- **Light version:** Tier 2 work. Do Step 1 and the day-90 check only.
- **Skip it:** Small updates that don't change what anyone says or does.

If your team has its own rules for sources or claims, use them alongside this skill.

---

## Step 1: Set the target (before any work starts)

Fill in one plan per project. Aim for a first pass in 15 minutes. It's fine to leave fields open, as long as each open field has an owner and a date.

| Field | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Project | The name and one line on what it is | New reporting dashboard launch |
| Motion | Sales-led, self-serve, or mixed | Sales-led |
| Why this matters | The company goal it supports | Company goal: grow mid-market revenue 25% this year |
| The change we want | What should be different afterward, in one sentence | Mid-market buyers stop choosing Competitor X over us for reporting |
| Who has to change | The people whose behavior should shift | Mid-market reps, buyers in active deals, existing customers |
| Main number | One number that shows the change happened | Win rate against Competitor X in mid-market |
| Who owns the main number | The team or person accountable for it | Head of mid-market sales |
| Who sets the target | PMM with their manager, or another team (inherited) | PMM with manager |
| Starting point | Where that number is today | 31% (last 2 quarters) |
| Target | Where it should be, and by when | 40% by end of Q2 |
| Early signs | 2–4 signs that fit your motion, including at least one usage sign (see below) | Reps use the "reporting in 5 minutes" line on calls; buyers ask for the dashboard demo; fewer "reporting gap" loss reasons; 30% of mid-market accounts open the dashboard within 60 days |
| Can we measure it today? | Yes / partly / no, the fallback for now, and the date full tracking is ready | Partly: call and deal data is ready; dashboard usage event ships Feb 20, so until then use weekly active accounts |
| Where the evidence lives | The exact tool for each sign | Gong for calls, Salesforce for deals and loss reasons, Highspot for asset sends, Pendo or Amplitude for usage |
| Other things that could move the number | Holidays, partner launches, other teams' promotions, betas, pricing changes, competitor moves | Competitor X pricing change rumored for March; sales kickoff in February |
| Comparison plan | How you'll tell whether PMM's work made the difference (see below) | Roll out to the East region first; compare East to West |
| Launch date | The date check-ins count from; mark it if it's tentative | Feb 1 (confirmed) |
| Check-in dates | Days 30, 60, and 90, each moved to the nearest recurring manager meeting | Mar 3, Apr 1, May 5 (Tuesday 1:1s) |
| What PMM owns | Your part | The message, demo script, battlecard, and rep training |
| What PMM doesn't own | Parts other teams own, so a shared number isn't claimed as PMM's | Pricing, the dashboard itself, rep hiring and quotas |

### Early signs by motion

- **Sales-led:** message used on calls, demo or meeting requests, asset sends in active deals, loss reasons, plus one product-usage sign.
- **Self-serve:** one sign per funnel stage PMM's work moves, such as sees the page → starts → finishes setup → uses it again. The last stage counts as the usage sign.
- **Mixed:** one or two sales signs, one or two funnel signs, and a usage sign.

### Comparison plan options

- **Sales-led:** roll out to one region, segment, or team first, then compare it to the rest. Or compare deals that used the work against deals that didn't.
- **Self-serve:** hold back a small group with a feature flag, show the new page or message to part of the audience first, or compare before and after, with a note about seasonal changes.
- **When none of these is possible:** use before and after, and say plainly that it shows a link, not proof.

### Inherited targets

Sometimes another team owns the main number and sets the target, such as Product, Growth, a partner, or a leader. When that happens:
- Record whose target it is and when it's due, and write "PMM adopts this target; it doesn't set it."
- Set PMM's early-sign targets with your manager. Those are the targets PMM is accountable for.
- If the owner hasn't set a target yet, mark it open with them as the owner.

### Open fields

Mark any field you can't fill yet like this:

```
OPEN — owner: [name or team], due: [date]
```

Then list every open field in the "What we need before we can agree" block at the end of the template. The plan isn't final until that block is empty, or until your manager agrees to start with the gaps listed.

**Rules for Step 1:**
- One main number only. If you can't pick one, the goal isn't clear yet.
- The starting point has to be real data, not a guess. If the data doesn't exist, mark it open and make collecting it the first task.
- If "Can we measure it today?" is "no," setting up tracking comes before launch work. Note the fallback you'll use until it's ready.
- If PMM sets the target, set it with your manager, not alone, so it isn't too easy or too hard. If it's inherited, follow the rules above.
- Name the other things that could move the number now, not at the readout. Anything named later looks like an excuse; anything never named gets claimed as PMM's.
- If you can't fill in "why this matters," question whether the project should happen at all.

### Check-in timing

- Count days 30, 60, and 90 from the launch date. If the date is tentative, say so, and update the check-ins when it's confirmed.
- Move each check-in to the nearest recurring manager meeting, so it actually happens.
- If a check-in falls near a holiday or a slow period, note it in "Other things that could move the number."
- If a metric needs a longer window than the check-in (for example, a 30-day repeat rate can't finish by day 30), note what that check can't show yet.

## Step 2: Log the evidence (while it's live)

Add an entry whenever you see a sign. Spend five minutes a week, on the same day each week.

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Date | Feb 12 |
| What happened | Rep on the Acme deal used the "reporting in 5 minutes" line, and the buyer asked for a second demo |
| Type | Message used / Deal moved / Deal lost / Funnel moved / Product used / Product changed / Leader decision / Outside factor / Tracking change |
| Source | Link to the Gong call |
| Deal value (sales-led only) | $48K, or "—" for self-serve |
| Linked to which early sign | Sign 1: reps using the message |

**Where to look each week:**
- **Call recordings:** Search for 2–3 exact phrases from the new messaging.
- **CRM:** Check deals won or lost against the main competitor, and the reason given for each loss.
- **Content tool:** See which assets were sent, and in which deals.
- **Funnel and product analytics:** Check each funnel stage you're tracking, and whether usage holds steady.
- **In-app surveys, support, and CS tickets:** Look for questions, confusion, or praise about the feature or message.
- **Slack and email:** Look for reps or CS asking questions about the topic, or sharing wins.
- **Product and leadership meetings:** Note any decision that cited your research or messaging.
- **Outside the company:** Note holidays, partner launches, competitor moves, or other teams' promotions as they happen.

**Rules for Step 2:**
- Log losses and drops too, not only wins. Leadership trusts a log that shows both.
- For every lost deal, write down the real reason, not just the CRM dropdown choice, along with the dollar value.
- When sharing loss data with Product, group it across many deals ("6 losses, $210K, same reason"). One big deal is a story, not a pattern.
- Log outside factors when they happen, even if you named them in Step 1, so the timing is on record.
- Log tracking changes, such as a new event going live, a broken event, or a definition change, so later numbers aren't misread.
- If two weeks go by with no entries, that's a finding in itself: either the work isn't landing or you aren't looking in the right places.

## Step 3: Monthly rollup (across all projects)

On the first business day of each month, starting with the first month that has data, send leadership one short update covering every active project.

> **PMM impact, March (sales-led):** 4 projects live. PMM work was used in deals worth $1.2M in open pipeline. Deals that used it won 9 points more often than deals that didn't. On track: 3. Behind: 1 (pricing page, low usage; fix in progress).

> **PMM impact, March (self-serve):** 3 projects live. Setup completion for users who saw the new onboarding rose from 42% to 51%; the comparison group held at 43%. On track: 2. Behind: 1 (upgrade page; tracking event ships Mar 18, so no data yet).

Then add one line per project with its main number and early signs, marked as on track or behind. That's it.

### Template: monthly rollup

```
PMM IMPACT, [MONTH]:
[#] projects live.
Sales-led: PMM work was used in deals worth $[X] in open pipeline.
  Deals that used it won [Y] points more often than deals that didn't.
Self-serve: [funnel stage] for users who saw PMM's work moved from [A] to [B];
  the comparison group was at [C].
On track: [#]. Behind: [#] ([project], [reason]; [fix]).

- [Project]: main number [start → now], early signs [summary] — on track / behind
- [Project]: ...
```

## Step 4: Check against the target (days 30, 60, 90)

| | Day 30 | Day 60 | Day 90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main number | 33% | 36% | 41% |
| Early sign 1: message on calls | 20% | 45% | 55% |
| Early sign 2: demo requests | 6 | 14 | 22 |
| Early sign 3: "reporting gap" losses | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| Usage: accounts opening the dashboard | 12% | 24% | 33% |
| Other things that happened | Sales kickoff | — | Competitor X raised prices |
| Can't show yet | 60-day usage target | — | — |
| On track? | Early, slow | Yes | Target hit |
| What I'm changing | Retrain the West team, whose usage lags | None | Expand to enterprise |

### Day 30: Is the problem the product or the message?

| What you see | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| People hear the message and are interested, but don't use the feature | Product | Share the usage data with Product, and pause new marketing until it's fixed |
| People who find the feature use it, but few find it (reps don't mention it, or users don't see it) | Message delivery | Retrain reps, move the asset into tools people already use, or move the in-product surface |
| People see the message and aren't interested | Message | Go back to customer interviews and rewrite |
| Nothing moving anywhere | Too early, the wrong target, or tracking isn't working | Check the "Tracking change" log entries, then talk to your manager about whether to keep going |

### How to read the main number

- **Early signs moving, main number flat:** It's probably too soon, so keep going.
- **Early signs flat:** The message or asset isn't reaching people. Fix how it's delivered before rewriting it.
- **Main number moving, early signs flat:** Something else is driving the change. Check the outside factors you named and logged, and say so honestly; don't claim it.

### Shared numbers

When Sales, Growth, Demand Gen, or another team also touched the result, compare the people who saw your work against those who didn't, using the comparison plan from Step 1. Report it as "deals (or users) that saw X did Y% better," not "PMM drove $Z."

Note that most comparisons show a link, not proof: stronger reps may use more content, and more motivated users may find more pages. A holdout or test group is the strongest evidence when you can set one up.

## Step 5: Write the readout (one page, every quarter)

This goes to your manager, the CMO, or the CEO. Make it the standard PMM update at quarterly business reviews, so it becomes routine rather than a one-off.

1. **Why it mattered:** The company goal it supported.
2. **What we set out to change:** One sentence, plus the target and whose target it is.
3. **What happened:** The main number from start to now, whether you hit the target, and usage.
4. **Proof:** Three log entries with links. For sales-led work, use one call, one deal, and one decision. For self-serve work, use one funnel change, one piece of user feedback, and one decision.
5. **How sure we are:** The comparison you used, and how each outside factor named in Step 1 or logged in Step 2 may have helped or hurt.
6. **What didn't work:** One honest miss and what you changed because of it.
7. **What's next:** The next target, or what you're stopping.

### Example readout: sales-led

> **Why:** Supports the company goal of growing mid-market revenue 25% this year.
>
> **Goal:** Win more mid-market deals against Competitor X on reporting (31% → 40% by end of Q2). Target set by PMM with the VP of Product Marketing.
>
> **Result:** 41% at day 90. Target hit. 33% of mid-market accounts now use the dashboard.
>
> **Proof:** (1) Acme call where the buyer asked for a second demo after hearing the new reporting line, $48K, won. (2) The "reporting gap" loss reason dropped from 4 deals to 1. (3) Product moved scheduled exports up a quarter after win/loss showed 6 losses worth $210K for the same reason.
>
> **How sure we are:** We rolled out to the East region first. East's win rate rose 10 points in that time; West rose 2. Competitor X raised prices in March, as we flagged in the plan, which likely helped both regions.
>
> **Miss:** The West team was slow to use the message. A 20-minute retraining at day 30 fixed it.
>
> **Next:** Same approach for enterprise, with a target of 25% → 33% against Competitor Y.

## Common mistakes this skill prevents

- Reporting what you built ("12 assets, 3 trainings") instead of what changed.
- Choosing the target after the results come in, or choosing it alone.
- Treating an inherited target as PMM's own, or setting a target you can't measure yet.
- Using sales signs for a self-serve project, or the reverse.
- Claiming a shared number as fully PMM's, or presenting a link as proof.
- Naming outside factors only after results come in, or never naming them at all.
- Logging only good news.
- Blaming the message when the product is the problem, or the reverse.
- Running projects that don't connect to a company goal.
- Waiting until review season to piece together evidence from memory.
