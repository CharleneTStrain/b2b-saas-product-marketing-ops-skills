# Rep-Ready Content Loop

**What it does:** It makes sure every sales asset is built for a real moment in a deal, sits where reps already work, and stays accurate, so reps actually use it and it helps win deals.

**When to use it:**
- **Full version:** Battlecards, objection guides, talk tracks, demo scripts, one-pagers, pricing guides, case studies, ROI tools, and launch kits.
- **Light version:** A small update to an existing asset. Do Steps 3 and 4 only.
- **CS and support content:** Customer enablement one-pagers, onboarding guides, renewal talk tracks, and support macros. See "Using it for CS and support content" below.
- **Skip it:** Content that isn't used in a live conversation with a buyer or customer, such as blog posts or awareness campaigns.

This skill works inside whatever tools you already have, such as your CRM, call recorder, Slack, or sales content tool. It sets what goes in them and how they're kept up to date; it doesn't replace them.

If your team has its own rules for sources or claims, use them alongside this skill.

---

## Step 1: Pick what to fix first

Don't start with a list of assets. Start with the deal moments that cost the most.

1. Pull the last two quarters of win/loss notes and lost-deal reasons.
2. Group the losses by the moment they went wrong, such as a competitor objection, a pricing pushback, or a security question.
3. Add up the dollar value lost at each moment.
4. Start with the top one or two moments. Work down the list from there.

| Moment | Deals lost | Value lost | Content today | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer compares our reporting to Competitor X | 9 | $410K | Old battlecard, not used | 1 |
| Pricing pushback in late stage | 6 | $260K | None | 2 |

**Rule:** If a moment isn't costing deals, it doesn't need new content yet.

**Before there's data** (a pre-launch product, a new feature, or no win/loss history), rank by one of these instead:
- The stuck points expected in the impact plan, such as the funnel stage or objection the team thinks will cause the most drop-off.
- An order agreed with the sales or CS partner.

Mark the ranking **"Proposed, no data yet,"** and re-rank at day 30 once real losses, tickets, or usage come in.

---

## Step 2: Ask reps (by topic, not per asset)

Run one round of interviews per topic or competitor, and use it to build every card in that set. Talk to three or four reps for 20 minutes each, including at least one top performer and one newer rep.

**Questions to ask:**
1. "Walk me through the last deal where [topic] came up. What happened?"
2. "What did the buyer say, word for word, if you remember?"
3. "What did you say back? What do you wish you'd said?"
4. "Where did you look for help, and did you find it?"
5. "When in the deal does this come up: first call, demo, pricing, or late?"
6. "If you had one line or one fact in front of you right then, what would it be?"
7. "What do you send buyers after this comes up?"
8. "What do you use now that you trust? What have you stopped using, and why?"

Then fill in one brief per asset.

| Field | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | The name and one line on what it is | Competitor X battlecard |
| Type | Rep-facing (reps read it) or buyer-facing (reps send it) | Rep-facing |
| The moment | When in the deal it's needed | Demo, when the buyer says "Competitor X has better reporting" |
| What the buyer says | Their real words, from interviews or calls | "X's dashboards look a lot more polished." |
| What the rep needs | The one thing that changes what they say or send next | A comeback, one proof point, and a question to ask |
| Where reps look | The tool they'd have open at that moment | Call recorder sidebar, CRM opportunity page |
| Who it's for | Which reps or segments | Mid-market AEs |
| Source of truth | Where the facts come from | Win/loss notes, product docs, competitor's public pricing page |
| Product checker | The PM who confirms feature claims are true today | PM for reporting: [name] |
| Owner | Who keeps it accurate | PMM: [name] |
| Sales partner | The sales leader who agrees it's needed and owns adoption | Mid-market sales manager |
| Review cadence | How often it's checked (see Step 5) | Weekly (top-three competitor) |

**Rules for Step 2:**
- Build for one moment per asset. If it covers five moments, split it into five cards.
- Use the buyer's real words, not marketing's version of them.
- If reps already have something they trust for this moment, improve it instead of building a new one.
- If no sales leader agrees it's needed, question whether to build it at all.

## Step 3: Build it so it can be used in 10 seconds

### Rep-facing card (for content reps read)

```
WHEN THEY SAY: [the buyer's real words]
YOU SAY: [one or two sentences, in a rep's voice]
PROOF: [one fact, number, or customer example, with source]
ASK: [one question that moves the deal forward]
DON'T SAY: [the line that backfires, if there is one]
LAST UPDATED: [date] · OWNER: [name] · PRODUCT CHECK: [PM name, date]
```

**Example:**

```
WHEN THEY SAY: "X's dashboards look a lot more polished."
YOU SAY: "They do look great. The question is how long it takes your team
  to build one. Ours takes about five minutes, with no analyst needed."
PROOF: Acme's ops team cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 20 minutes
  (Acme case study, 2026).
ASK: "Who on your team builds reports today, and how long does it take them?"
DON'T SAY: "Their dashboards are just pretty." Buyers hear it as dismissive.
LAST UPDATED: Feb 10 · OWNER: [PMM name] · PRODUCT CHECK: [PM name], Feb 9
```

### Buyer-facing card (for content reps send)

Case studies, ROI tools, one-pagers, and comparison pages get a cover note the rep sees before sending:

```
SEND IT WHEN: [the moment, in the buyer's words]
WHO IT'S FOR: [buyer role and segment]
THE ONE THING IT PROVES: [one sentence]
WHAT TO WRITE WHEN YOU SEND IT: [two or three sentences the rep can copy]
LAST UPDATED: [date] · OWNER: [name] · PRODUCT CHECK: [PM name, date]
```

The asset itself should make its main point on the first screen or page, so a busy buyer gets it without reading the whole thing.

### Checks before it ships

1. **Rep voice check:** A top rep reads the "You say" or "What to write" lines out loud. If they'd change a word, change it.
2. **Product accuracy check:** The product checker confirms every feature claim is true today, after the rep's rewrite. Anything planned but not shipped gets cut or marked "coming in [quarter]" with Product's agreement.

**Rules for Step 3:**
- One screen, no scrolling. If it doesn't fit, it's two cards.
- If a line doesn't change what the rep says or sends next, cut it. Pricing history, funding rounds, and executive bios belong in PMM's notes, not on the card.
- Every proof point needs a source a rep can name if a buyer asks.
- Include an honest "where they're stronger" line on competitive cards. Reps trust cards that admit a weakness.
- No card ships without both checks.

## Step 4: Put it where reps already are

**The click test:** Time how long it takes a rep to find the asset during a mock call. If it takes more than 30 seconds, move it.

**Where to put it, in order of preference:**
1. **Inside the tool reps have open in the moment,** such as a call recorder sidebar, a CRM opportunity field, or a deal-stage prompt.
2. **One search away,** in Slack with a pinned message or a bot, or in your sales content tool tagged by deal stage and competitor.
3. **One click from a bookmark,** as a single linked page, not a folder.

**Rules for Step 4:**
- One home per asset. If a card lives in three places, two of them will go out of date.
- Tag every asset by deal stage, competitor, and segment, since that's how reps search.
- Announce new or changed assets where reps already talk, with one line on the moment it's for and a link. Skip the email with a PDF attached.

## Step 5: Check and refresh

### Refresh cadence

| Content | How often |
|---|---|
| Top two or three competitors | Weekly, and within two days of a launch, price change, or major news |
| Other competitors | Monthly |
| Product claims | Within five business days of any release that changes them, triggered by release notes |
| Everything else | Quarterly |

### Monthly check (first business day of each month)

| Check | Where to look | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Is it helping? | Win rate and deal speed in deals that used it, compared with deals that didn't, using the [content-to-deal linker](../pmm-impact-tracker/content-to-deal-linker.md) | Report this first; feed it into the PMM impact tracker |
| Is it being used? | Content tool opens and sends, CRM attachments, call searches for the "You say" lines | Low use: check where it lives before changing what it says |
| Is it accurate? | Competitor sites, release notes, pricing changes, win/loss notes | Update it, get a new product check if claims changed, and change the "last updated" date |
| What are reps saying? | The monthly feedback slot or async thread | Log each request, act on it, and tell reps what changed |

### Rep feedback

**First choice:** five minutes in a monthly sales team meeting. **Fallback** if the time isn't given: a monthly Slack thread with the same three questions, posted by the sales partner so reps take it seriously.

1. "What did a buyer say this month that you didn't have an answer for?"
2. "What's on a card that's wrong or out of date?"
3. "What worked that other reps should know about?"

### Sorting rep requests

| Request type | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Content is missing, wrong, or hard to find | PMM fixes it |
| Buyers keep asking for a feature we don't have | Log it, group it across deals with the dollar value ("5 deals, $180K, same gap"), and send it to Product monthly; one request alone isn't a pattern |
| A question about pricing, legal, or security terms | Route it to the owner of that area |

**Rules for Step 5:**
- **Retire anything unused for 90 days,** unless it's for a rare but important moment, like a security review. Archive it; don't leave it sitting there.
- **Low use usually means bad placement,** not bad content. Move it first, then rewrite.
- **Stop rule:** If an asset still isn't used 60 days after being moved and rewritten, retire it, and stop building that type of asset for that moment until reps ask for it.
- **Close the loop on every rep request,** even when the answer is no. Reps stop giving feedback when it disappears.

### Template: asset register

| Asset | Type | The moment | Where it lives | Owner | Sales partner | Product check | Last updated | Next review | Uses last 30 days | Win rate with / without | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | Rep / Buyer | | | | | | | | | | Not started / Blocked (on: ___) / On hold / Live / Fix / Retire |

**Statuses:**
- **Not started:** agreed, not yet built.
- **Blocked (on: ___):** can't move until something else happens, such as a claims answer or a product decision. Name what it's waiting on and who owns that.
- **On hold:** paused on purpose. Note why and when to revisit.
- **Live / Fix / Retire:** as above.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | Content / Feature gap / Other owner | | |

## Using it for CS and support content

The same loop works for content CS managers and support teams use with existing customers: build it for a moment, put it where they work, then refresh and retire it. These are the differences:

| Step | Sales version | CS and support version |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pick what to fix first | Lost deals, by moment and dollar value | Churned or downgraded accounts, support ticket volume by topic, and failed setups or integrations |
| 2. Ask | Reps, including a top performer and a new rep | CS managers and support agents, including one who handles the hardest accounts and one who's newer |
| 3. Build | "When they say / You say" cards | "When the customer asks / Here's how" cards: the answer, the steps or link, and when to escalate. Buyer-facing cards become customer-facing guides |
| 4. Place | CRM, call recorder, Slack | The help desk or ticketing tool, the CS platform, saved replies or macros, the help center |
| 5. Check | Win rate in deals that used it | Ticket resolution time, repeat tickets on the same topic, setup completion, renewal or expansion in accounts that used it |
| Partner | Sales leader | CS or support leader |

The rest carries over unchanged: the product accuracy check, the refresh cadence, the 90-day retire rule, the stop rule, and closing the loop on every request.

### Template: CS and support card

```
WHEN THE CUSTOMER ASKS: [their real words]
HERE'S HOW: [the answer, in one or two sentences, in a CS manager's voice]
STEPS OR LINK: [numbered steps, or a link to the help article]
ESCALATE WHEN: [the condition, and who to escalate to]
LAST UPDATED: [date] · OWNER: [name] · PRODUCT CHECK: [PM name, date]
```

**Example:**

```
WHEN THE CUSTOMER ASKS: "Why isn't my data syncing from the integration?"
HERE'S HOW: "Most sync issues come from a permission that wasn't granted during
  setup. It takes about two minutes to fix."
STEPS OR LINK: 1. Settings → Integrations → [App]. 2. Click "Reconnect."
  3. Approve the "read data" permission. Help article: [link]
ESCALATE WHEN: Reconnecting doesn't fix it within an hour, or the error says
  "account locked." Escalate to Tier 2 support.
LAST UPDATED: Mar 4 · OWNER: [PMM name] · PRODUCT CHECK: [PM name], Mar 3
```

### Template: CS asset register

| Asset | Type | The moment | Where it lives | Owner | CS or support partner | Product check | Last updated | Next review | Uses last 30 days | Measure with / without | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | Agent-facing / Customer-facing | | | | | | | | | e.g. setup completion, repeat tickets | Not started / Blocked (on: ___) / On hold / Live / Fix / Retire |

## Shared ownership with Sales

Agree on this split with sales leadership up front:

| PMM owns | Sales leadership owns |
|---|---|
| What the content says, keeping it accurate, where it lives, and the product accuracy check | Whether reps use it, coaching reps on it in deal reviews, and the monthly feedback slot or thread |

Then agree on two or three numbers both teams watch, such as the share of competitive deals with the battlecard opened, and win rate in deals that used it compared with deals that didn't.

**Quarterly joint review:** PMM and the sales leader look at those numbers together for 30 minutes each quarter. Decide what to fix, move, or retire, and who's coaching which team. This keeps adoption from quietly becoming PMM's job again.

## Common mistakes this skill prevents

- Building content for what marketing wants to say instead of the moments that are losing deals.
- Interviewing reps for every single asset instead of once per topic.
- Putting content in a library reps have to leave their workflow to find.
- Packing every fact onto one card until nobody can scan it.
- Shipping talk tracks that don't sound like a rep, or rewrites that overpromise the product.
- Letting competitive content or product claims go stale.
- Rewriting content that's unused because nobody can find it.
- Reporting opens and sends instead of deals.
- Keeping every asset forever, or building more of a type that never gets used.
- Sending Product one-off feature requests instead of grouped patterns.
- Asking reps for feedback and never telling them what changed.
- Making PMM solely responsible for whether reps use the content.
