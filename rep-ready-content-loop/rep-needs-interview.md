# Rep Needs Interview

Part of the [rep-ready content loop](SKILL.md). Step 1 of the loop: find out what reps need in the moment, before building anything.

Most unused content was built for what marketing wanted to say, not what reps needed mid-deal. Twenty minutes with three or four reps fixes that before any work is wasted.

---

## Before the interviews: pick what to fix first

Don't start with a list of assets. Start with the deal moments that cost the most.

1. Pull the last two quarters of win/loss notes and lost-deal reasons (see the [win/loss revenue tagger](../pmm-impact-tracker/win-loss-revenue-tagger.md)).
2. Group the losses by the moment they went wrong, such as a competitor objection, a pricing pushback, or a security question.
3. Add up the dollar value lost at each moment.
4. Start with the top one or two moments. Work down the list from there.

| Moment | Deals lost | Value lost | Content today | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer compares our reporting to Competitor X | 9 | $410K | Old battlecard, not used | 1 |
| Pricing pushback in late stage | 6 | $260K | None | 2 |

**Rule:** If a moment isn't costing deals, it doesn't need new content yet.

**Before there's data** (a pre-launch product, a new feature, or no win/loss history), rank by one of these instead:
- The stuck points expected in the impact plan, such as the funnel stage or objection the team thinks will cause the most drop-off.
- An order agreed with the sales or CS partner.

Mark the ranking **"Proposed, no data yet,"** and re-rank at day 30 once real losses, tickets, or usage come in.

---

## Run the interviews (by topic, not per asset)

Run one round of interviews per topic or competitor, and use it to build every card in that set. Talk to three or four reps for 20 minutes each, including at least one top performer and one newer rep. Top performers show what works; newer reps show what's confusing.

**Questions to ask:**
1. "Walk me through the last deal where [topic] came up. What happened?"
2. "What did the buyer say, word for word, if you remember?"
3. "What did you say back? What do you wish you'd said?"
4. "Where did you look for help, and did you find it?"
5. "What did you search for and not find?"
6. "When in the deal does this come up: first call, demo, pricing, or late?"
7. "If you had one line or one fact in front of you right then, what would it be?"
8. "What do you send buyers after this comes up?"
9. "What do you use now that you trust? What have you stopped using, and why?"

Write down the buyer's exact words and the rep's search terms. They become the "When they say" line on the card and the tags in the [content finder audit](content-finder-audit.md).

## Fill in one brief per asset

| Field | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | The name and one line on what it is | Competitor X battlecard |
| Type | Rep-facing (reps read it) or buyer-facing (reps send it) | Rep-facing |
| The moment | When in the deal it's needed | Demo, when the buyer says "Competitor X has better reporting" |
| What the buyer says | Their real words, from interviews or calls | "X's dashboards look a lot more polished." |
| What the rep needs | The one thing that changes what they say or send next | A comeback, one proof point, and a question to ask |
| What reps searched for | The words they typed or asked for | "competitor X reporting," "X vs us dashboards" |
| Where reps look | The tool they'd have open at that moment | Call recorder sidebar, CRM opportunity page |
| Who it's for | Which reps or segments | Mid-market AEs |
| Source of truth | Where the facts come from | Win/loss notes, product docs, competitor's public pricing page |
| Product checker | The PM who confirms feature claims are true today | PM for reporting: [name] |
| Owner | Who keeps it accurate | PMM: [name] |
| Sales partner | The sales leader who agrees it's needed and owns adoption | Mid-market sales manager |
| Review cadence | How often it's checked (see the [freshness cycle](freshness-cycle.md)) | Weekly (top-three competitor) |

## CS and support version

Pick what to fix first from churned or downgraded accounts, support ticket volume by topic, and failed setups or integrations. Interview CS managers and support agents, including one who handles the hardest accounts and one who's newer. Swap "buyer" for "customer" and "deal" for "account" in the questions. The partner is the CS or support leader.

---

## Rules

- Build for one moment per asset. If it covers five moments, split it into five cards.
- Use the buyer's real words, not marketing's version of them.
- If reps already have something they trust for this moment, improve it instead of building a new one.
- If no sales leader agrees it's needed, question whether to build it at all.
- Interview once per topic, not once per asset.
