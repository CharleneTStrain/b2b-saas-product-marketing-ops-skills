# Launch Timeline Review

Part of the [shared launch agreement](SKILL.md). Step 4: after each launch, find where it stalled, and cut or overlap those steps next time.

Launches stretch to 12–16 weeks mostly from waiting, not working: waiting on approvals, waiting on product details, and redoing assets after a late change. This review finds the waits and removes them, one launch at a time.

---

## When to run it

- **Day 45 after every Tier 1 launch,** after the [consistency check](consistency-check.md). If day 45 falls on a holiday or weekend, move it to the nearest working day before it.
- **Once a quarter** for Tier 2 launches, reviewed together.
- **Before you set up the launch process map,** on your last two or three launches, to see where time goes today.

---

## Steps

### 1. Rebuild the timeline (30 minutes)

Using the kickoff agreement, decision record, threads, and calendar, list every step from kickoff to launch day, with the date it started and the date it finished.

**This only works if the dates were logged as they happened.** Log handoff starts and ends in the [decision record](decision-record.md) during the launch. If they weren't, estimate, mark the row "estimated," and log them properly next time.

### 2. Split working time from waiting time

For each step, ask: "How many of those days was someone actually working on it?" The rest is waiting.

### 3. Tag each wait

| Tag | What it means | Usual fix |
|---|---|---|
| Waiting on product details | Marketing or Sales couldn't start until Product shared what it does | Move it earlier, into the kickoff agreement |
| Waiting on approval | A review sat in someone's queue | Set a review deadline; silence after the deadline counts as approval |
| Rework | An asset was redone after a late change | Lock the decision earlier; log it in the decision record |
| Disagreement | Two teams couldn't agree | Use the tie-break rule's four-day clock |
| Done one after another | Steps waited on each other when they didn't have to | Run them side by side |
| Late scope change | What shipped changed after assets started | Agree "Reopen only if" at kickoff |
| Keep | A step that went faster than usual | Write down why, and make it the default next time |

### 4. Pick one or two fixes

Don't fix everything at once. Pick the one or two waits that cost the most days, and change the [launch process map](launch-process-map.md) for the next launch.

### 5. Track the trend against the target

Keep a running row per launch, so you can see whether the fixes are working. Compare each launch with the target launch length agreed with leadership (see "The target" in the [skill overview](SKILL.md)), and update the [business case](business-case.md) with the result.

## Rules

- **Count waiting days, not just total days.** The total hides where the time went.
- **Fix one or two things per launch.** A full process overhaul after every launch never sticks.
- **Don't add review rounds to fix a slow launch.** More reviewers almost always means more waiting.
- **Run steps side by side** wherever one doesn't need the other's output.
- **Share the trend with the three leaders each quarter,** so the fixes stay agreed.
- **Measure every launch against the agreed target,** including the ones that missed it.
